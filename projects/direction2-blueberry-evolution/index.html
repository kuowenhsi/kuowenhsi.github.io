Tracking evolution through time

Using blueberry genetics and historical collections to test responses to climate change.

Direction 2 · Future research

Can wild plant populations evolve in response to climate change within a century?

I will connect knowledge from crop breeding with wild populations and historical herbarium specimens to test for genetic changes consistent with recent adaptation.

Why blueberry? ↓Research approach ↓Testing for evolution ↓

A native fruit crop as a window into adaptation

Highbush blueberry spans broad climatic gradients in eastern North America. Its recent domestication and extensive breeding resources offer a way to connect climate-related traits with candidate genomic regions.

What breeding can tell us

Cultivars vary in flowering time, chilling requirements, and climatic tolerance. These differences provide starting points for identifying genetic variation relevant to environmental responses.

What wild populations can reveal

I will test whether candidate variants occur naturally, follow climatic gradients, and have changed in frequency over time.

Some breeding-associated variants may come from other Vaccinium species. I will first establish which variants exist in wild highbush blueberry and evaluate interspecific gene flow.

Three sources of evidence

  1. 01 · CROP GENETICSIdentify candidates

    Use breeding and functional studies to prioritize regions associated with climate-relevant traits.

  2. 02 · WILD POPULATIONSMap present-day variation

    Sample climatic gradients and test genetic and phenotypic differences in common gardens.

  3. 03 · HERBARIUM COLLECTIONSLook back in time

    Compare historical and modern DNA from well-sampled localities.

Link genetic differences to plant performance

Whole-genome data will characterize population structure, diversity, demographic history, and gene flow. Clonal propagation will allow the same genotypes to be tested under contrasting conditions, connecting genetic variation to flowering and other climate-related traits.

Recover evidence from historical specimens

I will audit herbarium records to identify suitable places and time periods for comparison. Because historical DNA is often fragmented, I will pilot targeted sequencing of candidate regions alongside neutral loci that help account for demographic change.

What would support recent adaptation?

If climate-associated variants shift through time more than neutral variation in the same localities, the result would support recent selection as a hypothesis. Population history, structure, and gene flow will be considered when interpreting those shifts.

The goal

Test whether wild blueberry populations show genetic changes consistent with adaptation to recent climate change, while identifying variation that may support conservation and future crop improvement.
