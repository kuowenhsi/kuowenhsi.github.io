<section id="blueberry">
<h2>A native fruit crop as a window into adaptation</h2>
<p>Highbush blueberry spans broad climatic gradients in eastern North America. Its recent domestication and extensive breeding resources offer a way to connect climate-related traits with candidate genomic regions.</p>
<div class="research-grid">
<div class="research-panel"><h3>What breeding can tell us</h3><p>Cultivars vary in flowering time, chilling requirements, and climatic tolerance. These differences provide starting points for identifying genetic variation relevant to environmental responses.</p></div>
<div class="research-panel"><h3>What wild populations can reveal</h3><p>I will test whether candidate variants occur naturally, follow climatic gradients, and have changed in frequency over time.</p></div>
</div>
<p class="research-note">Some breeding-associated variants may come from other <em>Vaccinium</em> species. I will first establish which variants exist in wild highbush blueberry and evaluate interspecific gene flow.</p>
</section>
<section id="approach">
<h2>Three sources of evidence</h2>
<ol class="research-steps" aria-label="Three complementary sources of evidence">
<li><span>01 · CROP GENETICS</span><strong>Identify candidates</strong><p>Use breeding and functional studies to prioritize regions associated with climate-relevant traits.</p></li>
<li><span>02 · WILD POPULATIONS</span><strong>Map present-day variation</strong><p>Sample climatic gradients and test genetic and phenotypic differences in common gardens.</p></li>
<li><span>03 · HERBARIUM COLLECTIONS</span><strong>Look back in time</strong><p>Compare historical and modern DNA from well-sampled localities.</p></li>
</ol>
<h3>Link genetic differences to plant performance</h3>
<p>Whole-genome data will characterize population structure, diversity, demographic history, and gene flow. Clonal propagation will allow the same genotypes to be tested under contrasting conditions, connecting genetic variation to flowering and other climate-related traits.</p>
<h3>Recover evidence from historical specimens</h3>
<p>I will audit herbarium records to identify suitable places and time periods for comparison. Because historical DNA is often fragmented, I will pilot targeted sequencing of candidate regions alongside neutral loci that help account for demographic change.</p>
</section>
<section id="evidence">
<h2>What would support recent adaptation?</h2>
<p>If climate-associated variants shift through time more than neutral variation in the same localities, the result would support recent selection as a hypothesis. Population history, structure, and gene flow will be considered when interpreting those shifts.</p>
<div class="research-note"><h3>The goal</h3><p>Test whether wild blueberry populations show genetic changes consistent with adaptation to recent climate change, while identifying variation that may support conservation and future crop improvement.</p></div>
</section>
